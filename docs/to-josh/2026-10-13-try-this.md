# Try this, and see whether it does what you asked

Seven minutes, in one session. Each one is a thing you said was wrong — say the thing on the left,
and the right-hand column is what should happen. If any of them doesn't, that's a fault and worth
telling me about rather than working around.

You don't need to know any commands. Say it the way you'd say it to a person.

---

## First, 30 seconds: make sure your session can see the new tools

**Ask:** "what writing mode is this in?"

**Expect:** `one_step`, and a sentence saying nothing will ask you questions or pull from the idea
bank.

If it says it doesn't know, or starts interviewing you instead, **your Claude session is holding an
old list of the tools** — it cached it before Tuesday's changes went up. The system is current; your
client's copy of its menu isn't. Disconnect the content system and reconnect it, or just start a
fresh chat, and ask again.

This is worth 30 seconds because it happened to me while testing: the server had the new path and my
session couldn't see it, so it quietly did the old thing. Every test below would have "failed" for
that reason alone.

---

## 1. It should not ask you a single question

**Say:** anything that happened this week. Half a sentence is enough.

**Expect:** it tells you what it filed the idea as, and stops. No question, no "tell me more about",
no counter. If it asks you a question, that's the complaint still live.

## 2. It should write about what you just gave it

**Say:** "write that one up."

**Expect:** a post built from the words you just said. Nothing from the idea bank, nothing from
September, nothing you have to approve the choice of first.

The test that matters: the post should be recognisably about *your* thing. On the 13th you answered
21 questions about one idea and got a draft about a different one from a month earlier. That cannot
happen now — the four-hourly job that queued that idea does nothing while this mode is on, and the
write path no longer reads the queue at all.

## 3. A post should reach you even when the checks don't like it

**Expect:** after it writes, it reads you what the eight checks caught, and then hands you the post
anyway. Something like *"two of eight flagged — the hook gives the ending away, and one phrase is on
your banned list. It's yours to judge."*

That morning, four checks failed and you got silence. Now the flags are information, not a gate. A
failure is still recorded as a failure — the moment that stops being true there's no way to tell
whether any of this is working — but what happens next is your call.

## 4. A number you gave should be in the post, or named as missing

**Say:** something with a figure in it. "We took it from 40 hours a week down to 6."

**Expect:** either the figure is in the post, or it tells you it couldn't carry it — by name, before
you read the draft. If it says a number was dropped, say "put the 40 back in" and it can fix it on
the spot.

This is the one you found yourself. Both write-ups of your TAM interview dropped every figure you
gave — 10 years, six months, tens of thousands a year, 30%, under $16 — and nothing said so. A post
can only state what the entry holds, so those were gone. That check now runs on the automatic
write-up too, which had never run any checks at all.

**One honest limit:** "tens of thousands" still isn't detected. The detector understands "thousand"
but not "tens of", and teaching it would make it cry wolf on every vague quantity. Figures with a
unit, a currency sign or a percent are covered.

## 5. Something too thin should come back as a sentence, not silence

**Say:** a fragment with nothing in it. "Had a good call yesterday."

**Expect:** one line telling you there isn't enough in it to write from yet, and what's missing. Not
a post, and not nothing. Add to it whenever you like and it picks up where it left off.

## 6. Your library should be straight with you

**Ask:** "what's in my library?"

**Expect:** **13 of 14 sections filled**, with the voice section named as empty.

Until today it said 14 of 14. The voice section reported itself as 1,205 characters of content; the
content is a note *to you* asking you to record one, and the thing writing your posts was being told
"not supplied, work without it". You were reading a full library while the writer had a hole in it.
That's why drafts kept failing the voice check — there was nothing to check them against.

While you're there: the step that turns your answers into material was running with **no library at
all**, from a one-word mistake. All 21 of those answers were processed by something that had never
read your pillars, your audience or your rules.

## 7. It should give you the interview back if you want it

**Say:** "turn the questions back on."

**Expect:** it does, immediately, and the interview works exactly as it did. Then say "stop asking me
questions" and it stops again.

You said "for the time being", so nothing was deleted. Every idea, answer and draft you have ever
given it is still there, and the gate goes back to holding posts back the moment you switch.

## Two things are sitting there waiting for you

**Two drafts are in your calendar** and nobody has read them to you — the TAM ones from Tuesday
morning, both with two of eight checks flagged. Four posts in total are waiting on a verdict.

The useful thing isn't yes or no. It's **marking one up**: which line is wrong, which line is yours,
where it stops sounding like you. The acceptance test in the contract is three in a row you'd post
with light edits, and a marked-up rejection teaches this thing more than an approval does.

## The one thing I still need from you

**Fifteen minutes of you talking — making a case for something, not answering questions.**

The voice section is empty, which the system has now admitted for the first time, and it's the single
biggest reason drafts come back sounding like anyone. Six recorded calls exist and in five of them
your job was to ask the questions, so it knows how you ask and not how you argue. The opening and the
close are exactly what a conversation can't show you.

Paste a transcript into a session and say "add this to the voice interview".

## And one thing you should know rather than discover

That's deliberate until you've seen three you'd stand behind.
