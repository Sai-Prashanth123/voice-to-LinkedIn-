## Your hook document contradicted your own rules

Worth knowing because it was costing you posts. Banned phrases has been your **worst-performing
check — 23 of 36 runs since 4 November, 64%**.

Your list says: *Formulaic reveal: "Let me explain." / "Here's why." / "Here's what happened." Just
say it.* Your document is built on exactly that move — premise, setup, then a curiosity trigger —
and carries about fifty of them as a swipe file, including three the list bans by name.

On the Vercel post you mentioned, that rule failed the opener *"The small print on Vercel cutting
its SDR team from 10 to 1:"* — while the hook check **passed the identical line on the same run**.
Two checks, opposite verdicts, one sentence.

**Resolved by position, not by permission.** A trigger earns its place when the lines above it carry
a real premise and setup that it pays off, and stays banned when it opens cold or announces nothing.
Measured: of the 2,082 reference posts that kept their line breaks, **31 open with a bare trigger —
1.5%**. Those writers almost never open with one; what they do is earn it first.

It still fails what should fail. *"Here's how I build outbound from data you already have:"* has no
premise above it — no number, nothing at stake — and it still fails, as it did four times in
November.

So: either the rule is still too tight, or that opening was not your best work and the check would

## Point 1 — news and opinion

The check now has a third way to pass: **your position, with something at stake in it.** A take on
what the field published passes when the post commits you to a judgement you would defend, with a
consequence named for your buyer at their altitude. The test it applies is *what would it take for
this post to be wrong* — if nothing could make it wrong, nothing was staked.

A plain recap still fails. So does a recap with an opinion bolted on, which is the thing the new
wording is most likely to be misread as allowing, so it says so in those words.

You were right about handover test 8 as well: its stated reason was the pre-31-October rule. The
examples still fail; the reason now matches what the check actually asks.

## Point 2 — it can add things now

You asked to be checking additions rather than blocked by them, so: the writer may add reasoning,
argument, an implication or a general example, and each one carries a one-line reason. **They are
read back to you before you approve** — in the reply when the draft is filed, and again at the top
of your review list, above the post.

Four things are still refused outright, and nothing is stored if one appears: a figure of any kind
(including a written-out one — "three ways" is a quantity), a quotation, an uncleared name, and any
account of something you did, saw or were told.

That fourth one is wider than the three you named, and deliberately. Your document teaches rage
hooks and says outright *"do you actually have to do these things? No, we're being sarcastic."* A
fabricated anecdote in your voice is one you would have to check against your own memory rather than
review — so the system can make an argument you did not make, and not claim an experience you did
not have. The tension those hooks create is available from contrast and from stakes, both of which
are in your hooks section and both of which are true.

> "I've found that even with a waterfall, you're looking at a **15-20% drop in accuracy on mobile

and the model's own note on it said `[his_number]`. I searched every note you have given this
system, every summarised field and the receipts: **that figure appears nowhere you have ever said

A draft could not have done this — the claim ledger refuses an unsourced number outright. A comment
had no ledger at all, and the code said so in its own header: *"they cannot catch an invented
number."* It was right, and the first real run produced exactly that.

There is now a fourth check on comments: a figure that appears neither in your material nor in the
post being replied to is flagged. **It is weaker than it sounds and you should know by how much.**
Your material holds 241 distinct figures, and against that comment it catches "60" and nothing
else — "15" and "20" both appear somewhere in your notes in unrelated contexts, so "a 15-20% drop"
passes on its digits. It caught that one by one number out of three. Only a per-comment ledger could
do better; a whole-corpus match cannot tell "15 appears in his material" from "15% of mobile numbers
decay".

**I have struck that option.** It is recorded as discarded, not deleted. The other two are still
waiting for you and both are clean.

### Four of your six creators had never been read once

This is the one I would least like to have missed, and the comment options above are what led to
it.

Julia Carter and Jen Allen-Knuth were switched on at 13:56 on 18 November and the 15:02 run polled

Raising the timeout was not available: it is already four minutes and the isolate dies well before
that. The run had to get smaller.
