## 2. The contradiction. Yes, and the line we'd draw is attribution rather than wording

Your read is correct, and I want to be straight about one thing before you decide. The rule isn't
only about the phrasing; it was written against the construction, and it says so itself: *"The fix
is not to reword it. Delete the rejected half entirely and state Y once, with a detail attached."*
It also warns that the softened forms "do the same work three sentences later and are the ones that
get through". So narrowing it to a list of banned strings would leave a ban the cadence walks
straight around.

What does hold the useful half of your move is **who owns the belief**:

> **The exception, and the only one: an assumption with an owner.** "The CFO told me the number was
> fine" names a belief and challenges it, and it is a scene, because a person held it. What stays
> banned is the ownerless version — "most people think", "everyone believes", "the common view is" —
> and every softened form of it. The test is not the wording. It is whether a reader could ask who
> believed this and get an answer from the post.

That keeps challenging an assumption up front, which is what you asked for, and it keeps the rule's
teeth. It also happens to match what your eight writers do: they open on something specific 45% of
the time and on a number about themselves 26%, and a consensus opening is the rarest shape in the
whole corpus — 5 posts out of 156. So attribution isn't a loophole to lean on. It's the difference
between a scene and a cadence.

That's **proposal 16**, waiting on you. It's the whole section with that one block added and nothing
else touched — 7,967 characters to 8,757, head and tail byte-identical, because this is the text
your gate judges every draft against and we weren't going to retype it.
