Josh — all four are done. Two of them had already shipped before we spoke and I can now show you
they are working. The other two turned out to be more interesting than either of us thought on the

## The lived-experience rule, and the claim check

Both were loosened on Friday night. You ran six posts through on Monday morning, and across those
six the lived-experience check failed **none** of them. It had been failing 42% before. One of your
drafts passed all nine checks.

The check now passes a post anchored in **work you did** rather than only a room you sat in — you
read the paper against your own outbound, you ran the numbers, you tried the method and it broke.
What still fails is a recap with an opinion bolted on, which I think is the line you wanted.

There is a second half you asked for that is new this morning: the brief now tells the drafter what
of yours is on file for a given post, and **says plainly when there is nothing** and to write it
anyway. Your words were that it should flag it but not script against it. That is what it does now.

On the claim check — it is doing better but it is still the strictest thing in the system, and I
want to be straight with you about one of Monday's failures, because it was right. The post said a
110% lift came from the list built off the booking-page method. Your own notes say the reps were
working the earlier, smaller version in October, so the method came *after* the result. That is
worth one clause rather than a looser check.

What I did change: the gate is now told **how much material you gave it**. Where you dictated three
paragraphs it holds the post to all of it; where you said "write me a post about X" it stops
demanding that every sentence trace back to words you never said. Quotes, numbers, names and scenes
are judged exactly as strictly either way — a thin brief is a reason to write a thinner post, never
a licence to invent the missing detail.

## Hooks

This is the one where I owe you a correction. Your hook rules are already excellent — 3,677
characters, and they name your exact complaint: *"No gap at all. The opening states its
conclusion."* The rules were never the problem. Three things downstream of them were.

**Nothing in the system measured the first line.** The structure check read the first *three* lines
together, so a long opening sentence followed by two short ones scored the same as three even lines.
The word count of line one has been sitting in the database on every reference post since the
beginning and reached nothing. It now does, with the band those eight writers actually sit in.

**Two of your detectors were refusing the hook you asked for.** You said this on the call, out
loud — *"Since doing this one thing, they've improved 10x. Here's why."* The system refuses
`Here's why`. Your banned list rules out `"Here's why."` as a line that stands alone and explains
nothing, which is right; the code was matching those words anywhere, including as a teaser into the
sentence immediately after. Measured across your eight reference writers, that one rule alone would
have refused **279 of their posts**. It now refuses the form your list names and reports the rest.

**Your worked example had dropped out of the brief.** Three times in this project you have rewritten
a line rather than rejecting it, and those three are the only positive examples the system has of
what better looks like. The brief read the ten most recent notes; your first rewrite is now
fifteenth, because your own activity pushed it down. So all three had quietly fallen out. They are
back, and they are pinned so recency cannot lose them again.

The one you rewrote, for the record, was: *"My holidays used to have client meetings in them. Now I
say no."* You cut the second sentence — the one that answered your own hook.

### The swipe file, and why it is not 150 posts

We said 150 to 200 openings. I have built it from **1,839** instead, and it carries no sentences —
only structure: how long a first line runs by shape, how often the finding is withheld past line
one, how often they name the reader.

That is not a shortcut, it is a requirement. Your system refuses any draft sharing a seven-word run
with a stored reference post. A file of real openings would be a field of landmines: the better the
drafter followed it, the likelier the draft would be refused. There is also precedent — an
8,287-character read of Matt Barker once shipped on every brief and quietly became the default
structure you complained about.

One finding from building it, which cuts against your own rules. Your hook section asks the opening
to carry specificity, a real emotion and clear stakes. Measured, your eight reference writers carry
two or more of those in **under one first line in ten**. Specificity is common; the other two are
rare. I have put the number in the brief as a reported fact rather than a target, because turning it
into a requirement would fail nine in ten of the writers you picked as models. Your advice about
what makes a reader care still stands. It is just not what these eight measurably do.

## Repetition — you are half right, and half the opposite

**The half you are right about.** The rule that refuses a draft for repeated bullets fires on
**46%** of your reference writers' posts and would have refused **129** of them outright, including
55% of Matt Barker's. The refusals were things like *four bullets starting "what"* — a list of
parallel questions, which is a device, not a tell. It now refuses only when the repeated word
carries meaning. Across the three strictest rules, refusals on your reference corpus went from 444
to 79, and the reporting did not change at all: you still see everything, it just stops blocking.

**The half that is the other way round.** The check you watched run — the one comparing against
previous ideas — has been comparing against almost nothing. It needs an embedding for each idea.
There are 122 ideas and **six** embeddings, and the published archive is empty, so it has been
returning "clear" honestly and uselessly.

What got through: on 29 October, **four posts for four different ideas all opened with the identical
sentence** — *"The number of reps who don't want to cold call shocks me."* On 13 October, three did
the same. Nothing in the system noticed, because nothing compared a draft against other drafts.

There is now a check that cannot be starved — it compares the first line and the seven-word runs
against your own earlier drafts, needs no AI service at all, and advises rather than refuses, since
repeating yourself is your call.

## The preview

You pasted a post into a preview tool on the call to see the wall of text. The system could already
render that and was not showing you — it was buried in an optional tool. Every draft now comes back
with the text a reader sees before "see more".

**One thing I still need from you, and it is about forty taps.** That preview is simulated: we
assume a line holds about 42 characters and the cut comes after three. That is an estimate and it
moves with your phone and your text size. If you open forty of the reference writers' posts on your
phone and tell me the last word you can see before "see more", I will fit it properly. Until then I
will not quote you a word count as if it were measured.

## Your headshot

It works. I tested it properly rather than assuming — rendered the same image twice, once with an

**What I need is a small file.** The image has to be embedded in the graphic itself, which costs
