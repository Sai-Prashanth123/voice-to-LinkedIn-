## Fixed

**A rejected draft can be rewritten.** The dead end is gone. Both your stuck ideas have been
rewritten and filed, every claim traced back to something you actually said. The three-strike park
is what stops a runaway loop, not that refusal.

**The gate judges the post, not your notes.** It is now told, in its own instructions and in the
three checks that read your material: judge the post, quote the words from the post that fail, and
if you cannot quote them there is nothing to fail. Specifically:

- An omission is not a fabrication. Your post said "one specific account" without naming it. That is
  anonymising, which is what we asked it to do.
- A name only fails if the post identifies a real person by it. It failed you on "Ben" — the wrong
  name a stranger put at the top of a cold email to you.
- Your draft is now a permanent test. If anyone ever loosens this, that test fails first.

**The judging moved to where the strong model is.** The eight checks were being made by the cheapest
model Google sells, because no paid API key has ever been in this project. They now run in Claude,
through the same connector that writes the drafts, at no extra cost. The trade is honest: a draft
filed on Saturday is not judged until someone opens Claude. One setting puts it back on the server.

**The sentinels are live again.** All seven accounts re-scraped: 112 posts, 52 of them new since the
last measurement. Two things in the new scrape would have quietly corrupted it, and both are now
guarded — a profile scrape returns the subject's whole feed, so other people's posts were being
measured as new accounts; and this scrape stripped the line breaks, which makes every measure of
shape read the whole post where it means to read one line. Those measures now rest only on the posts
that kept their breaks, and say so.

**Matt Barker is read properly**, since that is what you asked to lean into. It is waiting for your
approval as one library proposal, and it says plainly that two of his most recognisable moves cannot
transfer: his turn renames the reader's problem, which is the exact construction your banned-phrases
list forbids, and he closes on an ask in all six of his latest posts where your own rule rations that
to one in five. There is a fork in there for you to settle.
